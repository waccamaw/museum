For a nation of hundreds, the Waccamaw constitution builds a genuinely sophisticated
machine. The 2016 text lays out **three separate branches**:

**Executive** — a Tribal Chief (with genealogical ties to the Dimery Settlement,
elected at large, four-year term), a Vice Chief elected as running mate, and up to two
appointed Second Chiefs. The Chief runs everything *external* and holds a **veto**
over Council decisions — overridable only by five of seven Council votes.

**Legislative** — a seven-seat **Tribal Council** plus an elected Chief of Tribal
Council who runs meetings and agendas. The Council owns everything *internal*. Seats
are staggered: odd seats elected in U.S. presidential years, even seats two years
later alongside the Chief.

**Judicial** — two **Tribal Judges** (disputes between members, or member vs.
government), an Associate Judge, and a seven-member **Council of Elders** who review
every ordinance for constitutional conflict and run **all elections**.

Then the safeguards:

- **The three-readings rule** — nothing affecting the general membership passes until
  it's been read three times in open meetings. The archive is full of resolutions with
  three dated reading lines and an attested vote tally; when the tribe waived the rule,
  it passed a *resolution to waive it* (HH-10-07-2011-001) — and the waiver got its
  own paper trail.
- **Initiative and referendum** — thirty percent of registered voters can force any
  question onto a ballot, over the government's head.
- **Vote-by-anywhere** — rules for online and phone votes were adopted back in 2009
  (DH-01-09-2009-001); by 2026 the Council was routinely voting by email, each tally
  read into the record by the Chief.

Constitutional design is usually studied in nation-states. It's worth seeing what it
looks like when a community of a few hundred people does it for themselves, carefully,
over twenty-five years.

*Sources: Constitution of the Waccamaw Indian People (2016), Art. V–VII, X;
HH10072011001; DH01092009001; decision records 2026, Waccamaw governance corpus.*
