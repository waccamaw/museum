The Waccamaw Indian People are **South Carolina's first state-recognized tribe**, recognized by the State of South Carolina in **2005**. In the tribe's own words:

> The Waccamaw Indian People are South Carolina's first state-recognized tribe (2005), with over 300 years of continuous presence in the Dog Bluff community near Aynor. We preserve our cultural heritage, maintain tribal sovereignty, and support our community through educational programs, cultural events, and traditional practices.

## More than 300 years at Dog Bluff

For over 300 years, the Waccamaw people have called the **Dog Bluff community** near **Aynor, South Carolina**, home. The tribal grounds are located at 591 Bluewater Rd, Aynor, SC. The tribe describes itself as a people with "a proud civil rights tradition we intend to carry forward."

## A living culture

The Waccamaw Indian People work to preserve their cultural heritage and maintain tribal sovereignty, supporting the community through educational programs, cultural events, and traditional practices. As the tribe puts it, their annual gathering "celebrates our living culture and welcomes visitors to experience authentic indigenous traditions."

Community efforts described on the tribe's site include cultural programs and classes, tribal meetings, youth education and Youth Days, school days, and community support services such as food bank and mobile pantry distributions.

## The Annual Pauwau & Cultural Arts Festival

Each year the tribe hosts the **Waccamaw Pauwau & Cultural Arts Festival** on the Waccamaw Tribal Grounds in Aynor. The **34th Annual Pauwau** is scheduled for **November 7–8, 2026**. The Pauwau is an open celebration of the tribe's living culture and a welcome to visitors.

## Culture, kept by law

Between the budgets and the bylaws, the tribe's own resolutions keep writing its
culture into law: a **storyteller designated by resolution** (2006) — an oral-tradition
office created by written instrument; **Waccamaw Day**, established 2009, the tribe's
own holiday on its own calendar; the **Pauwau committee made a standing, year-round
body** with its own tribal sub-account (2006–07); a committee for the **Woccon
language** of the ancestors; even a policy for **memorial tree plantings**. Culture is
what a people does — the record is where a people commits in writing to keep doing it.

## The old ways, remembered

From the chief's 2014 oral history, a register of tradition as lived practice:

- **"Making meat."** *"We didn't have a word that meant hunt. Our word meant making
  meat, and we felt like the only reason to take a life was to sustain life."*
- **The potato bank.** His father preserved winter food the traditional way — holes
  lined with broom straw, dug in several places *"because if you got an animal in
  there you didn't want to lose everything you had… The dirt would insulate it from
  freezing. The straw would keep it from growing roots."*
- **Seashell graves.** Fourteen miles from the ocean: *"whenever you see a grave out
  and it's got a bunch of seashells on it, that's generally an Indian grave"* — a
  southeastern tradition the Waccamaw keep.
- **The turtle pipe.** The tribe's catlinite pipe, carved by John Cox, carries a
  turtle; bowl and stem are *"the blood of the ancestors and… the bones of the
  ancestors… you can't allow them to touch because if they ever touch it has to be
  smoked."*
- **The marble.** The chief's theology, in one image: *"everything that is alive is
  rolled into a big ball or marble… it doesn't matter how small of a piece it is, and
  it doesn't matter how far away from the anvil it flies. It's always a piece of God,
  nature."*

## Where they come from

The modern Waccamaw grew from the **Dimery Settlement** — 300 acres near Dog Bluff,
bought back in 1813 by John and Elizabeth Dimery, and held for a century by a handful
of families who were, in the era's cruel phrase, "neither Black nor White."

## "We Are Still Here"

The tribe continues to pursue **federal recognition** and to share its story publicly. A phrase the community uses to sum up its spirit: *#WeAreStillHere.*
