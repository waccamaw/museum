On **June 29, 2026**, at 3:45 in the afternoon, an email went out to the tribal
council and officers of the Waccamaw Indian People:

> *"I wanted to say to all who might care that today I rendered my resignation as the
> Chief of the Waccamaw. I am not angry or hurt."*

The arc starts long before the chieftaincy. A boy from a family of the Dimery
Settlement grows up an outsider in a segregated school system that — in the county's
own 1923 words — had no place for children "neither Black nor White." At seven or
eight, in a store in Cherokee, he feels for the first time what belonging is: *"here
was all these people that looked like me… I have a place to be now."* He carries that
feeling through Vietnam — **Bronze Star, Purple Heart, Vietnamese Cross of
Gallantry** — retires from the Army in 1988, and in 1992 builds the thing he'd been
looking for since that store: *"a place where people that have not been accepted into
the full society of this country can be accepted."* ([In His Own Words](/exhibits/in-his-own-words/))

**Chief Harold "Buster" Hatcher** led the tribe for roughly thirty years — the
2000 constitution and recognition petition, state recognition in 2005, the tribal
grounds, the courts and constables, Waccamaw Day, the federal petition. The documents
of that arc fill this archive; most exhibits in this wing are chapters of it.

The letter itself is the artifact that belongs in a glass case. It is 250 words long. It
thanks the tribe. It asks for loyalty to his successor — *"Please give your loyalty to
Chief Cail, I believe she will lead ably and honestly."* It attaches a copy of the
constitution and encourages members *"to use the tools in the constitution for any
problems they might face."* A soldier's handoff: he was raised in the Army, and says
so in the same breath.

Two structural details make the ending as clean as it reads:

**The succession was automatic.** Article VII.C.1 of the constitution: a vacancy in
the office of Tribal Chief "shall automatically be filled by the Vice Chief." No
special election, no interregnum. Cheryl Sievers-Cail — his Vice Chief, and per the
signature record already acting in the role since at least late 2024 — became Chief
the moment the resignation landed.

**The title outlives the office.** Under Resolution SH-08-04-2006-01, he retains
"Chief" for life — *with privilege but without responsibility and/or authority*. His
own gloss on it, in the letter: *"I will not comment one way or another on anything
pending in the tribe."* Title yes, thumb on the scale no.

He signed it *Harold (Buster) Hatcher, Chief Retired* — and in the constitutional
sense, that is exactly, precisely what he is.

*Sources: resignation email of 2026-06-29 (tribe-wide); Constitution (2016) Art.
VII.C.1; SH-08-04-2006-01; succession record, Waccamaw governance corpus.*
