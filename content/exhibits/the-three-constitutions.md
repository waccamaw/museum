Most people never see a constitution get written. The Waccamaw wrote theirs **three
times in sixteen years** — each generation a snapshot of what the tribe had learned
about governing itself.

**The 2000 Constitution.** Ratified by a vote of the Waccamaw People concluded on
**October 31, 2000** (the date survives in the saving clause of the current text).
This is the founding legal document of the modern tribal government — contemporary
with the tribe's formal push for recognition. The original text is still being
hunted in the archive.

**The 2008/09 Constitution.** A full revision **ratified in October 2008 and approved
in March 2009** — written in the immediate aftermath of the most turbulent period in
the tribe's modern governance, when the chieftaincy changed hands several times in
two years. Constitutions get rewritten when their gaps get found; this one reads like
a document that had just watched its own stress test.

**The 2016 Constitution** (revised September 8, 2016, then ratified) is the one in
force today. Three branches — an executive Chief with veto power, a seven-seat
legislative Tribal Council, and a judicial branch of two Tribal Judges plus a
seven-member Council of Elders. Staggered four-year terms timed against U.S.
presidential election years. A three-readings rule so nothing passes quietly. And
the people reserve **initiative and referendum** power to legislate over the
Council's head entirely.

The most elegant detail: **Article XI**, the saving clause, keeps every prior
resolution in force "to the extent that they are consistent with this Constitution."
Twenty-plus years of tribal law flowing forward through each rewrite — which is
exactly why the archive behind this wing exists: someone has to know what all those
resolutions *say*.

*Sources: Constitution of the Waccamaw Indian People (ratified 2016), Art. XI;
WIP Constitution ratified 10-2008/approved 03-2009 (tribal archive); constitution
lineage record, Waccamaw governance corpus.*
