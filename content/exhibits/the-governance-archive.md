The governance exhibits in this museum are drawn from a **verified archive of the
tribe's primary records** — constitutions, resolutions, council decisions —
assembled from the original scans and documents by the tribe's web team.

The method matters as much as the material:

- **Every record traces to its source.** Each resolution was OCR'd from the original
  scan, its operative text extracted verbatim, and the source document preserved and
  hash-anchored so the record can always be re-checked against the original.
- **Everything is independently re-verified** — including the handwritten
  strike-throughs and margin corrections on the old paper, which turn out to carry
  real history: hand-amended vote tallies, corrected dates, a chieftaincy
  reconstructed from signature blocks.
- **Every exhibit cites its documents** by resolution number, so any claim here can
  be traced back to the paper it stands on.

What these exhibits deliberately do *not* include: member records, private
correspondence, or matters the tribe keeps to itself. The full archive remains with
the tribe under its own classification rules; these pages are its cleared, public
surface — and as more records are cleared, the museum grows.

*These exhibits are curated from the records, not authored as the Tribe's official
history — that telling belongs to the Council, the Elders, and the storyteller.*
