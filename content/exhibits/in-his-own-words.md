Every line below is his, verbatim, from a recorded interview, a broadcast, or a letter
in the record. The museum adds only the order.

## The outsider

> "When I was in school, I didn't get along with the white people, because their
> cultures and my cultures were not the same… I had been an outsider everywhere I was,
> home or in school or whatever, my whole life."
> — *oral-history interview, 2021*

He was seven or eight when his mother took him to Cherokee — and in a store there, an
older Cherokee woman treated him as one of her own:

> "Here was all these people that looked like me… it made me feel like I'm home. This
> is where I'm supposed to be. I have a place to be now. **And that's the way I feel
> about the tribe… it's a place where people that have not been accepted into the full
> society of this country can be accepted.**"
> — *oral-history interview, 2021*

The line that carries the whole childhood — and the reason the tribe exists:

> "**I was wrong at home because I wasn't dark enough. I was wrong at school because I
> wasn't white enough.**"
> — *Center for Cultural Preservation oral history, 2014*

## The soldier

> "I've served time in Vietnam… Got a Bronze Star, Purple Heart, Vietnamese Cross of
> Gallantry."
> — *oral-history interview, 2021*

> "I was raised in the Army and all I know is Army Leadership."
> — *resignation letter, June 29, 2026*

He retired from the Army in 1988. Four years later, he founded the modern tribe.

## The chief

> "No great man became great by doing what his predecessors did. Great people see
> things that are wrong and fix them."
> — *SCETV, "Meet Harold Hatcher," November 2015*

## The son

His mother died around 2012, at ninety-eight. Federal law forbade a member of a
non-federally-recognized tribe from possessing an eagle's feather.

> "I placed an eagle's feather in her casket."
> — *oral history, 2014 — the burial-rights fight, in nine words*

## The goodbye

> "I have never lied to you, and never stabbed anyone in the back. I do nothing under
> the table… I don't perceive problems other than growing pains, and the disruption
> that any change of command would bring. Please give your loyalty to Chief Cail, I
> believe she will lead ably and honestly… Thank you for allowing me to serve in this
> AWSOME position. I did my best for you."
> — *resignation letter, June 29, 2026, signed "Chief Retired"*
